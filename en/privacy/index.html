02 / Photo processing in the app

FilmLab reads selected photos or RAW files on your device to preview, edit and export them. It processes image content and the file information, photo identifiers and metadata needed for those tasks. Images may contain people, locations or other personal information; local processing still involves data processing. The current version has no developer-operated upload service and no feature that automatically uploads photos, RAW files, projects or diagnostic files to the developer.

03 / Photos, Files and iCloud

The custom library accesses Photos according to system permissions. The system photo picker supplies items you select; Files import reads files you choose. The system may download Photos originals from iCloud, and file providers may retrieve files from cloud storage. Photos exports may sync according to your settings. System backups may also include local app data.

These system and file-provider transfers are separate from a developer-operated upload service and depend on your permissions, sync, backup and service settings. You can change permissions in system settings. FilmLab does not promise that every operation is offline or that all data always stays on one device.

04 / Local projects, sources and caches

To resume editing, the app stores projects, adjustments, work and batch records, thumbnails, imported user LUTs, Recipes and preferences locally. A source may be a Photos identifier or a managed copy inside app-owned storage. A managed copy is not an upload to the developer. RAW thumbnails support browsing; classification caches hold identifiers, revision or modification information, dimensions and types needed for classification. Reading and exporting can also create temporary files.

Managed originals may retain EXIF and other original metadata. Do not assume all metadata has been removed. External sources that have not been copied into managed storage may need to be selected again if access or provider availability changes.

05 / Work retention and deletion

Completed-work retention offers 1 day, 7 days, 30 days or forever. A valid existing setting takes priority; 7 days is the fallback only when no valid setting exists. Automatic expiry applies to inactive, expired work whose formal members have all exported successfully and whose imports are complete. It is measured from completion of export, not import, and project state is checked again before deletion. Unfinished drafts and active work do not share a blanket seven-day limit.

Manually deleting work removes its record and projects not shared with other work, and attempts to remove derived thumbnails; this operation conservatively retains managed source copies. Expiry cleanup also attempts to release app-owned managed copies after reference checks. Copies may remain for retry if other references exist, records cannot be fully read or cleanup fails.

“Forever” prevents new time-based expiry, but does not undo previously recorded deletions or copy cleanup. This rule does not delete Photos originals, external original files or shared user LUTs. Caches, temporary files, system backups and copies already sent to third parties have different lifecycles. Immediate, permanent deletion of all copies is not promised.

06 / Exports, sharing and the clipboard

You decide whether to export photos, share or copy a Recipe, and whom to send it to. A Recipe Code contains its name and editing settings, not the photo itself or an embedded imported LUT file. System sharing, clipboard sync and recipient services may further process the content you choose to provide. Check the content and recipient before sending.

07 / TestFlight testing and feedback

Use TestFlight’s built-in feedback for beta issues. Apple’s TestFlight service collects and provides developers with beta installation, usage, device and crash information. Feedback may also include screenshots, comments and related technical details. Email visibility depends on the invitation method and what you submit. This is separate from FilmLab having no third-party behavioral analytics or automatic crash-reporting SDK.

The publisher views necessary information to investigate and improve the app, without bulk-exporting all feedback by default. Apple independently retains platform data: its notice states one year for beta feedback and potentially until issues are resolved for crash and usage data. Developer-held copies follow the schedule below. See TestFlight & Privacy.

08 / Email support and optional attachments

For everyday support, email 634989219@qq.com. Sending email supplies the publisher with your email address, message, correspondence and any screenshots, samples or diagnostic files you voluntarily attach. Your email provider and the receiving QQ Mail service process that correspondence. The support mailbox is used only by the publisher and is not synced to other services; QQ Mail itself still processes and stores the correspondence. The website’s email link only opens your mail app; it does not send messages or attach photos automatically.

Start with the app version, device, operating system and steps to reproduce. Screenshots, RAW files and other samples are optional; a complete photo library is never required. Share only a minimal sample you have the right to provide, removing unnecessary people, locations, account details or other sensitive content. Originals may include EXIF. The app and website do not automatically read diagnostic files or upload feedback, samples or diagnostics to public issue trackers or third-party AI services. QQ Mail and the sender’s provider have their own storage, logging and backup rules; deleting app data does not delete email copies.

09 / Support retention and requests

Under the single-publisher operating policy, identifiable email, ordinary screenshots and necessary feedback records are kept for no more than 90 days after a case closes. Open cases with no substantive correspondence for 180 days are ended and the related records cleared. Unresolved cases are reviewed every 90 days.

Voluntary RAW files, original photos, projects and detailed diagnostic copies are deleted within 30 days after analysis finishes, and no later than 90 days after receipt. Further use requires the user’s agreement. Bug summaries may be kept for regression work only after contact details, images and identifying information have actually been removed. Only the publisher accesses support material for support and troubleshooting; access arrangements must be updated before adding another person.

Contact the support email to request access, correction or deletion of developer-held support material, or to withdraw permission for further use of optional attachments. Withdrawal does not affect earlier lawful processing. Only information needed to locate the records should be checked, never passwords, verification codes, private keys or a complete library. The publisher clears controllable mailbox, attachment and local copies; provider backups, logs and specific records that must legally be retained cannot be promised to disappear at the same time. Necessary exceptions should be narrow, explained and reviewed.

10 / Website hosting and access logs

The website uses GitHub Pages hosting. Its pages have no accounts, backend, forms, analytics or tracking scripts, third-party font CDN, cookies or local storage. Chinese and English use separate static addresses. The site does not automatically upload photos or diagnostics.

GitHub states that visitor IP addresses are logged and stored for security when a GitHub Pages site is visited, whether or not the visitor is signed in. Having no analytics script does not mean hosting involves no access data. GitHub controls its security logs and backups; this policy does not promise a particular retention period for GitHub.

See GitHub Pages data collection and GitHub’s privacy statement.

11 / Current services and policy updates

The first beta does not introduce ads, third-party behavioral analytics, a developer-operated upload service or an automatic crash-reporting SDK. Possible future services are not part of this release candidate. Before changing purposes, recipients, retention or services, assess the actual data flows, update both policy languages with the date and change notes, and provide notice or obtain any necessary consent as applicable before enabling that processing. Future plans do not describe current behavior.
